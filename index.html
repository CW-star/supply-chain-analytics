Key Findings

What the Data Reveals

Four critical insights — each pointing to a specific, quantified, actionable lever.

⚠ Shipping Crisis
95.3%

First Class shipping — the most expensive option — has a 95.3% late delivery rate across every market. Africa: 97%. Europe: 95.8%. Customers paying premium prices receive the worst service. Yet First Class remains profitable, creating a hidden long-term trust timebomb leadership cannot yet see in the P&L.

📈 Demand Shock
Oct 2017

The Prophet model expected 5,355 orders in October 2017. The actual figure was 2,255 — a 56.5% single-month collapse. Without the forecast model, this looks like gradual decline. With it, you see a precise event boundary, a date leadership can investigate, and a cause that can be identified.

📈 Revenue Gap
$1.6M

Across four post-shock months, the business lost $1,614,835 in expected revenue. November 2017: $411K gap. January 2018: monthly revenue at $332K — 68% below the pre-shock average of $1.04M. This is not an estimate. It is a modeled, month-by-month quantification.

💻 Structural Loss
Computers

9 of the 10 worst individual orders are Computers, with losses of −$2,255 to −$3,443 per order at discounts of only 0–12%. This is not a promotions problem. The product is priced or costed incorrectly. A pricing audit of this category is not optional — it is overdue.

My Insights
💡 My Insights from This Project
What I Learned &
What the Data Told Me
  • 1
    What I set out to answer
    I wanted to give supply chain leadership one complete picture: where are deliveries failing, what caused the 2017 revenue collapse, and which products are structurally unprofitable — not just describe the problem, but put a dollar amount on it.
  • 2
    What surprised me
    The October 2017 demand shock was completely invisible in raw order data. Only when I overlaid the Prophet forecast against actuals did the gap become visible and quantifiable. Without the model, you see "orders declined." With it, you see a precise $1.6M hole with a start date and a growth trajectory.
  • 3
    The uncomfortable business finding
    First Class generates profit despite failing 95% of its customers. The business has no short-term financial incentive to fix it — which means the problem will not fix itself. The right conversation is a customer lifetime value model that makes the long-term cost visible alongside the short-term margin numbers.
  • 4
    What I would do next
    Connect to live order data, build a real-time late delivery alert triggered at 48 hours past SLA, and add a CLV model that converts the 95% late rate from a service metric into a dollar-denominated risk that the CFO can act on.
